# Anchorage steers room tax money toward police patrols downtown

By [Zachariah Hughes](/author/zachariah-hughes)

Published: April 17, 2026

![](https://www.adn.com/resizer/v2/WPCS5HT5GNFJ7GCORHSQALMXXE.JPG?auth=945292b3661264ee8cbd79deea97ac0936e2ae97b60ee960134ef7b30ea62cb0&width=800&height=545)

A sign for the Anchorage Police Department on W. Fourth Avenue in downtown Anchorage on Wednesday, April 14, 2021. (Emily Mesner / ADN)

Anchorage officials are using tourism revenue to add more police around downtown as the city heads into the busy summer visitor season.

Beginning this winter, two Anchorage Police Department officers have patrolled downtown 14 hours a day, seven days a week in an effort to “promote a safe and welcoming environment for businesses, residents and visitors this spring and summer,” according to an announcement from Mayor Suzanne LaFrance on Tuesday.

The work is an overtime detail, making the labor hours more expensive than straight work time for the salaried employees.

“Using an overtime detail ensures that the foot patrol doesn’t pull officers away from regular patrols in other neighborhoods. This allows the Municipality to maintain usual law enforcement capacity while providing an extra layer of security downtown‚” Emily Goodykoontz, a spokesperson for the mayor’s office, wrote in an email Wednesday.

Though the patrols started in February to coincide with the Anchorage Fur Rendezvous festivities, they were designed to coincide with technical changes to city rules around disorderly conduct that went into effect April 8.

In February, the Anchorage Assembly approved a [measure from the mayor’s office](https://www.muni.org/Departments/Mayor/PressReleases/Pages/Anchorage-Assembly-approves-Mayor-LaFrances-ordinance-to-enhance-safety-in-public-spaces.aspx "https://www.muni.org/Departments/Mayor/PressReleases/Pages/Anchorage-Assembly-approves-Mayor-LaFrances-ordinance-to-enhance-safety-in-public-spaces.aspx") that gives police officers more latitude to contact people suspected of public intoxication, indecent exposure and threatening behavior.

For the last several weeks, said APD Police Chief Sean Case, officers have been talking with downtown businesses about the new rules, and how they can report problems and help officials build cases through security camera footage.

When the new rules on public conduct went into effect last week, Case said, officers shifted to carrying out more enforcement.

“We’re keeping pretty granular data every time one of our officers works one of these details,” he said Thursday.

Between April 8 and 15, APD officers downtown made contact 321 times with people while on patrol. In 182 of those incidents, Case said, there was “voluntarily compliance,” meaning they agreed to stop doing the offending behavior.

Four people were arrested for outstanding warrants, another for trespassing, and another for threatening conduct, according to Case. Thirty-four people were transferred to the Anchorage Safety Center, and four other people accepted referrals to social service providers.

Case said he’s heard criticism from different quarters about the new initiative. Some people want the police to arrest everyone observed violating public decency rules. Others don’t think law enforcement officers ought to be used to police complex social problems that show up on downtown streets.

Officers have to bid on overtime details, with seniority carrying weight in who gets the assignment. Case said that while some details are hard to fill, that is not so with this one, which has seen plenty of officers signing up.

“It’s kind of a fun detail, frankly,” Case said.

Officers rotate on and off throughout the shift, which lasts each day from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m.

The additional patrol shifts are paid for with money collected under a 4% tax on hotel stays. Residents approved that tax back in 1979, specifically for the “promotion of the tourism industry,” according to [memorandum](https://embed.documentcloud.org/documents/28049901-ar-2026-055-as-amended/?embed=1 "https://embed.documentcloud.org/documents/28049901-ar-2026-055-as-amended/?embed=1") filed to the Assembly in March. Traditionally, almost all of the bed tax money went to Visit Anchorage, the organization geared toward marketing the city and promoting tourism.

But at the end of last year, the Assembly approved a [measure](https://www.muni.org/Lists/AssemblyListDocuments/DispForm.aspx?ID=1335152&cmd=overridecb "https://www.muni.org/Lists/AssemblyListDocuments/DispForm.aspx?ID=1335152&cmd=overridecb") that steers a small portion of the broader bed tax to the municipality, earmarked for “tourism improvement projects,” or “TIPs.” For 2026, a total of $1.6 million in hotel room revenues is being allocated to the city for TIPs. From 2028 forward, about 10% of the bed tax will go to the city, the rest to Visit Anchorage.

Of that new revenue coming to the city, $650,000 will be used on the downtown patrols through the remainder of the year.

“Downtown is Anchorage’s living room. It’s often the first stop for commuters and tourists, and it’s where our residents, local businesses, and visitors gather and connect,” LaFrance said in a statement released by her office. “By putting officers on the ground, we are proactively addressing community needs and making downtown a safer and more welcoming place for everyone.”

The patrol units will loop in emergency medics, counselors and some of the specialty units the city has stood up within APD in recent years to work with people dealing with acute mental health, substance abuse and homelessness-related issues.

“If a patrol officer encounters someone in crisis or in need of specific services, they have a direct line to get those teams on the scene quickly,” Goodykoontz said.

The city will also spend some of this year’s TIP funds on management costs at the Alaska Center for the Performing Arts, supporting Fur Rendezvous activities downtown, and supplying “a mobile restroom facility to support tourism-facing facilities and events,” according to the appropriation [measure](https://embed.documentcloud.org/documents/28049901-ar-2026-055-as-amended/?embed=1 "https://embed.documentcloud.org/documents/28049901-ar-2026-055-as-amended/?embed=1") approved by the Assembly in March.

A spokesperson for Visit Anchorage did not return requests for comment on the new patrols.

The LaFrance administration has several projects underway to revitalize downtown Anchorage. Those include the opening of a new [library branch](https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/anchorage/2026/02/06/anchorage-plans-to-renovate-historic-city-hall-this-summer-open-downtown-library-in-2027/ "https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/anchorage/2026/02/06/anchorage-plans-to-renovate-historic-city-hall-this-summer-open-downtown-library-in-2027/"), [overhaul](https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/anchorage/2025/11/13/town-square-park-cameras-go-live-as-increased-citywide-shoplifting-patrols-set-for-black-friday/ "https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/anchorage/2025/11/13/town-square-park-cameras-go-live-as-increased-citywide-shoplifting-patrols-set-for-black-friday/") of two public parks, and [beautification efforts](https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/anchorage/2026/01/01/anchorage-neighborhoods-pursue-public-art-to-create-a-stronger-sense-of-place/ "https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/anchorage/2026/01/01/anchorage-neighborhoods-pursue-public-art-to-create-a-stronger-sense-of-place/") to make buildings more colorful and [less beige](https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/anchorage/2026/01/01/anchorage-neighborhoods-pursue-public-art-to-create-a-stronger-sense-of-place/ "https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/anchorage/2026/01/01/anchorage-neighborhoods-pursue-public-art-to-create-a-stronger-sense-of-place/").
