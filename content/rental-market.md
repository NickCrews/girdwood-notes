## Overview

The long-term rental market in [[girdwood]] is extremely tight, with very few units available and rents that are high relative to local wages. This is one of the most pressing issues facing the community and a major constraint on the local economy.

## Supply constraints

Several factors limit the supply of long-term rentals:

- **[[Short-term rentals]]**: Property owners can earn more from nightly vacation rentals than from long-term leases, incentivizing conversion away from residential use
- **[[Dark homes]]**: A large number of units are held as second homes and not made available for rent at all
- **Limited new construction**: Geographic constraints and the pace of new development limit housing growth
- **[[Heritage Land Bank]]** land disposals have been slow to produce new housing units

## Rent levels

Rents in Girdwood are high relative to local service-sector wages, creating an affordability gap for workers at [[alyeska-resort]] and other local businesses. Many workers commute from Anchorage rather than paying Girdwood rents.

## Impact on the community

The tight rental market directly affects:

- Worker recruitment and retention for local businesses (see [[girdwood-economics-and-labor-market]])
- Community demographics, as younger and lower-income residents are priced out (see [[girdwood-demographics]])
- The viability of seasonal worker housing

## Policy responses

Proposals to address the rental market include [[deed-restrictions]] requiring primary residency, STR regulation, and new workforce housing development on [[heritage-land-bank]] land.
