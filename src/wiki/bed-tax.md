## Overview

The bed tax (formally the **room tax**, also called occupancy, transient occupancy, or hotel tax) is a **12% tax on short-term lodging** levied across the [[municipality-of-anchorage]], including Girdwood. It is authorized by [Anchorage Municipal Code chapter 12.20](https://library.municode.com/ak/anchorage/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT12TA_CH12.20ROTA) and administered by the Municipal Treasury ([MOA Room Tax](https://www.muni.org/Departments/finance/treasury/programtaxes/roomtax/Pages/default.aspx)).

Two things make it the most important tax on this wiki after [[property-tax]]:

1. Girdwood's economy is lodging-heavy, so Girdwood generates bed tax far out of proportion to its population — but historically none of it came back to Girdwood as Girdwood money.
2. As of 2026 that changed at the margin. [[tourism-improvement-projects]] carves a slice of the tax away from Visit Anchorage and puts it in front of the [[anchorage-assembly]], and the [[girdwood-board-of-supervisors]] is now formally competing for it.

Room tax is **not subject to the municipal tax cap** ([2026 Approved GGOB, Revenues, p. III-2](https://www.muni.org/Departments/budget/operatingBudget/2026%20GGOB/2026%20Approved/Web%2003%20-%20Revenues.pdf)), which is why it keeps surfacing in every Anchorage fiscal debate.

## Who it taxes

The tax applies to any rental of **less than 30 consecutive days** and reaches essentially all lodging types: "hotels, motels, inns, corporate suites, bed and breakfasts, rooming houses, townhomes, cabins, duplexes, condominiums, vacation rentals, seasonal rentals and apartments" ([MOA Room Tax](https://www.muni.org/Departments/finance/treasury/programtaxes/roomtax/Pages/default.aspx)). So both [[alyeska-resort]]'s hotel rooms and Girdwood's [[short-term-rentals]] pay the same 12%.

The operator, not the guest, has the legal duty to collect and remit; returns are quarterly, due 30 days after quarter end. Operators must register with Treasury before renting, unless they rent exclusively through a registered hosting platform — under AMC 12.20.031, platforms like Airbnb and VRBO register with the Finance Department and remit on behalf of all their operators **in aggregate only**, which is the root of the municipality's long-standing inability to see STR-level data.

Notable exemptions under [AMC 12.20.022](http://anchorage-ak.elaws.us/code/coor_title12_ch12.20_sec12.20.022): stays of 30 or more consecutive days by the same person, rent paid directly by the federal or state government, employer-paid rotating crew housing occupied 30+ days, and hostel dormitory beds.

## How much it raises

| Year                 | Collections                                                  |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 2022                 | ~$40M (then a record)                                        |
| 2023                 | ~$45M, an all-time high                                      |
| 2024 approved budget | $39.6M budgeted (6.83% of total general-government revenues) |
| 2026 approved budget | $44.0M budgeted (7.00% of total revenues)                    |

Sources: [2024 Approved GGOB, Revenues](https://www.muni.org/Departments/budget/operatingBudget/2024%20GGOB/2024%20Approved%20Operating/Web%2003%20-%20Revenues.pdf); [2026 Approved GGOB, Revenues](https://www.muni.org/Departments/budget/operatingBudget/2026%20GGOB/2026%20Approved/Web%2003%20-%20Revenues.pdf); [Visit Anchorage bed tax FAQ](../sources/visit-anchorage-bed-tax-faq.md). Collections have averaged more than $26M annually since 2014, a figure dragged down by the COVID years. The 2026 budget note explains the flat-to-down forecast as "an anticipated overall decrease in travel for the Anchorage area in 2026."

For scale, this is the third-largest single revenue line in the general government budget, behind real and personal property tax.

### How much of that is Girdwood's?

**Unknown — the municipality does not publish room tax collections by geography.** This is the single biggest hole in the Girdwood fiscal argument and worth chasing.

Two indirect handles:

- The Treasury's fiscal note for [AO 2025-97](https://www.muni.org/Departments/Assembly/Documents/AO%202025-97%20Worksession.pdf) estimated that a **5%** levy on STR-only (non-hotel) transactions would raise about **$3.4M** municipality-wide in a full year. That implies an STR lodging base of roughly $68M/yr, and therefore roughly **$8M/yr** of the existing 12% room tax coming from STRs municipality-wide. (Arithmetic inference, not a published figure.)
- [[girdwood-board-of-supervisors]] told the Assembly that **~25% of Girdwood's residential properties** were in the vacation rental market in the 12 months through August 2025, versus **~1%** in the rest of the municipality ([GBOS Resolution 2025-18](../sources/gbos-resolution-2025-18-str-tax.pdf)). Girdwood is a small share of the municipality's housing stock but a very large share of its STR stock.

Neither gets you a Girdwood dollar figure, and neither counts [[alyeska-resort]]'s hotel rooms, which are almost certainly the largest single room tax generator in the valley.

## Where the money goes

The 12% is split into three equal 4% slices ([2026 Approved GGOB, Revenues](https://www.muni.org/Departments/budget/operatingBudget/2026%20GGOB/2026%20Approved/Web%2003%20-%20Revenues.pdf)):

| Slice      | Purpose                                                                     |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 4% (~$15M) | Debt service and other costs of the Dena'ina Civic and Convention Center    |
| 4% (~$15M) | Areawide general fund (fund `101000`, see [[muni-funds]])                   |
| 4% (~$15M) | Promotion of tourism — historically paid almost entirely to Visit Anchorage |

The tourism-promotion slice is **dedicated by a 1979 vote of the people** to "promotion of the tourism industry" ([AR 2026-55](../sources/ar-2026-55-tourism-improvement-projects.pdf)). That dedication is what makes the slice hard to redirect — it can't simply be swept into the general fund — and it is what shapes what [[tourism-improvement-projects]] money can be spent on.

The history, per Visit Anchorage's own account ([source](../sources/visit-anchorage-bed-tax-faq.md)): the tax was proposed in 1975 as a way for the visitor industry to tax itself to fund a destination marketing organization rather than compete for municipal, state, or federal money. In 1979 it rose from 5% to 8%, which is when the general fund started getting a cut. At the 2023 peak Visit Anchorage's share was about $14M.

Note that none of the three slices funds anything in Girdwood specifically. The [[girdwood-service-area]] is funded by property tax, not bed tax.

## [Tourism Improvement Projects](tourism-improvement-projects)

Since 2026 the municipality has withheld part of the tourism-promotion 4% from Visit Anchorage and spent it on Assembly-approved visitor-facing projects: $1.6M in 2026, $1.5M in 2027, and roughly 10% of Visit Anchorage's share annually from 2028 on ([AR 2026-55](../sources/ar-2026-55-tourism-improvement-projects.pdf)).

This is currently the only live mechanism by which bed tax generated in Girdwood could return to Girdwood, and [[girdwood-board-of-supervisors]] is assembling a 2027 ask. **See [[tourism-improvement-projects]] for the full picture** — how the pot works, what it may legally be spent on, where the 2026 money went, and Girdwood's position.

## Debate and alternatives

### The geographic-dedication fight (2025)

The bed tax debate in Girdwood is really an argument about **geography**: Girdwood generates lodging tax revenue and absorbs the housing cost of the industry that generates it, but the revenue is pooled municipality-wide.

That argument got its clearest test in late 2025. Assembly member Daniel Volland and Chair Chris Constant proposed [AO 2025-97](https://www.muni.org/Departments/Assembly/Documents/AO%202025-97%20Worksession.pdf), a ballot proposition for a Charter amendment adding a **5% tax on STR transactions only** (on top of the existing 12%), with net proceeds dedicated to housing and related infrastructure. Treasury estimated ~$3.4M/yr at full-year run rate.

[[girdwood-board-of-supervisors]] supported it **conditionally**. GBOS Resolution 2025-18 (passed 3-0 on October 20, 2025) backed the ballot proposition **"only if the revenue raised by STRs operating in the Girdwood area is dedicated to community housing projects in the Girdwood area"** ([resolution](../sources/gbos-resolution-2025-18-str-tax.pdf)). The resolution's reasoning is the sharpest statement of the Girdwood position on record:

> a 12% Room Tax on hotels and STRs is levied across the MOA, including Girdwood, but none of that revenue is dedicated to alleviate the resultant stresses on our community's workforce housing supply

Assembly member Zac Johnson, who represents South Anchorage and Girdwood, carried the corresponding amendment: spend STR tax revenue where it is collected "to the extent feasible," splitting the municipality into three zones — Girdwood and Turnagain Arm; the Anchorage Bowl; and Eagle River, Chugiak and Birchwood. "Building more homes in Anchorage does nothing for the community of Girdwood, where it is becoming all but impossible for working class families to live," Johnson argued. The counter, from Assembly member Erin Baldwin Day, was that housing money "will do more and better work if they are aggregated, as opposed to divided."

**The amendment failed, and then the whole ordinance failed 8-4** on December 16, 2025 ([ADN](../sources/adn-2025-12-17-assembly-str-tax-vote.md); [Alaska Public Media](../sources/alaskapublic-2025-12-17-assembly-nixes-str-tax.md)). Stated objections: too little reliable STR data to justify the tax, and bad timing against the mayor's competing 3% sales tax proposal.

The same meeting **did** pass AO 2025-115 (10-2), requiring STR owners to register with the Clerk's Office — which is the data-gathering precondition the skeptics said was missing.

### Where that leaves the argument

Two live theories of how Girdwood gets a return on the lodging tax it generates:

- **A new dedicated tax with geographic dedication.** Dead for now — the vehicle failed in December 2025 and would need voter approval via Charter amendment, since in Alaska this counts as a sales tax.
- **A share of the existing tax through [[tourism-improvement-projects]].** Alive, small, and annual. This is the pragmatic path, but it is discretionary, must be re-won every year, and is constrained by the "promote tourism" dedication — which means it funds _visitor-facing_ projects, not the workforce housing that GBOS actually argues the visitor industry is displacing.

### Other alternatives raised but not pursued here

- A **general room tax increase** was floated during the 2025 revenue debates alongside the 3% sales tax proposal ([Alaska Public Media](../sources/alaskapublic-2025-12-17-assembly-nixes-str-tax.md)).
- Comparable resort communities that levy an STR-specific surcharge dedicated to workforce housing, cited in AO 2025-97's own findings: Aspen (5-10%), Crested Butte (7.5%), Carbondale (6%), Telluride (2.5%), and Napa County / St. Helena, CA (1% each).
