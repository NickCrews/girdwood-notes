A fund is a pool of money that pays for things.
Note that this is distinct from a Fund Center (cost center), which actually is the thing doing the work.
Eg when Girdwood Parks & Rec pays for something, that is defined as $50 getting spent by fund center 558000 out of the 161000 fund.
This doc JUST talks about the fund numbers.

## The Anatomy of the 6-Digit Fund Number

* **Digits 1, 2, and 3 (Core Fund ID):** This tells you the *type* of fund and the *geographical service area* or department it belongs to. The very first digit is the most vital indicator of the fund's macro-category (e.g., General Government, Special Revenue, Utility).
* **Digits 4, 5, and 6 (Sub-Fund / Source Extension):** For standard, year-to-year operating budgets, these trailing digits are almost always **`000`**. If these numbers change (e.g., `800` or `300`), it typically points to a specific multi-year capital project improvement (CIP) extension, a reimbursable account, or a highly specific sub-funding source.

## Cheat Sheet: Given a Number, What is it?

You can instantly identify a fund's purpose by looking at the first digit and the core ranges:

| Fund Range | General Purpose | Major Anchorage Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **`101000`** | **Areawide General Fund** | Covers services benefiting the entire municipality (Libraries, Transit, Public Health, Planning, Animal Control). |
| **`102000` – `106000**` | **Specific Sub-Service Areas** | Services restricted to specific areas (e.g., `104000` Chugiak Fire, `106000` Girdwood Valley). |
| **`111000` – `129000**` | **Limited Road Service Areas (LRSA)** | Tiny hyper-local funds used strictly for neighborhood dirt road grading and local snow plowing (e.g., `111000` Birchtree/Elmore LRSA). |
| **`131000`** | **Anchorage Fire Service Area** | Fire protection and emergency medical rescue across the core Anchorage bowl. |
| **`141000`** | **Anchorage Roads & Drainage (ARDSA)** | Major street sweeping, snow removal, traffic lights, and storm drain maintenance in the urban bowl. |
| **`151000`** | **Anchorage Police Service Area** | APD operations, policing infrastructure, and dispatch. |
| **`161000`** | **Anchorage Parks & Recreation** | City greenhouses, trail maintenance, neighborhood parks, and recreational programming. |
| **`163000`** | **Building Safety Service Area** | Funded via building permit fees to handle code inspections and structural safety monitoring. |
| **`190000`** | **Operating Projects Committed Fund** | A special holding fund for general government operational money that has been saved or multi-year committed (like Mayor's community grants). |
| **`2XXXXX`** | **Special Revenue & Grants** | Money legally restricted to specific initiatives. Examples include **`206000`** (Alcohol Tax Fund) or **`221000`** (Heritage Land Bank). |
| **`4XXXXX`** | **Capital Improvement Projects (CIP)** | Used for major construction and infrastructure upgrades rather than daily operations (e.g., `451800` is Police Capital Projects). |
| **`5XXXXX`** | **Enterprise / Utility Funds** | Self-supporting municipal businesses funded by rate-payers rather than property taxes. Examples: **`540000`** (Water), **`550000`** (Wastewater), **`560000`** (Refuse Collection), and **`570000`** (Don Young Port of Alaska). |
| **`7XXXXX`** | **Trust & Fiduciary Funds** | Trust accounts held by the city, most notably **`730000`** (the MOA Trust Fund, built from the legacy sale of the telephone and utility assets). |

## The Inverse Guide: Given an Expense, Which Fund is it?

If you are looking at a real-world municipal expense or bill, ask yourself **who benefits** and **how is it funded** to pinpoint the correct bucket:

* **"We need to buy new books for the Loussac Library or fix a People Mover bus line."**
* *Logic:* Libraries and transit are used by everyone across the entire city boundary.
* *Fund:* **`101000` (Areawide General)**.


* **"We need to clear three feet of snow off Northern Lights Blvd or fix a traffic signal."**
* *Logic:* This is major road maintenance inside the primary Anchorage urban bowl.
* *Fund:* **`141000` (Anchorage Roads & Drainage Service Area)**.


* **"We are funding a substance abuse treatment clinic or a mobile crisis intervention team."**
* *Logic:* While this is public health, Anchorage voters passed a dedicated retail tax on alcohol to fund these specific initiatives.
* *Fund:* **`206000` (Alcoholic Beverages Retail Sales Tax Fund)**.


* **"We need to repair a water main break under the street."**
* *Logic:* Water infrastructure belongs to the city's utility enterprise, paid for by your monthly water bill.
* *Fund:* **`540000` (Anchorage Water Utility)**.


* **"A specific neighborhood in the hills needs their local cul-de-sac plowed."**
* *Logic:* This doesn't fall under major city road maintenance; it belongs to a hyper-local road service area agreement.
* *Fund:* Look in the **`111000` – `129000**` range depending on the exact neighborhood.
