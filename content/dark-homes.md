## Overview

"Dark homes" is a term used in [[girdwood]] to describe housing units that are not occupied as primary residences — including second homes, vacation properties, and units held for occasional use. The term refers to homes that sit dark and empty for much of the year.

## Scale

Census and utility data suggest that over half of Girdwood's housing units may qualify as "dark." This is one of the most striking features of Girdwood's housing landscape and a major contributor to the community's housing crisis.

## Impact

Dark homes have several effects on the community:

- **Reduced housing supply**: Units held as second homes are unavailable for full-time residents or long-term renters
- **Community cohesion**: A large stock of unoccupied homes reduces the sense of community and limits participation in local governance and civic life
- **Service costs**: The [[girdwood-service-area]] provides services (fire, road maintenance, etc.) to all properties regardless of occupancy, meaning year-round residents effectively subsidize services for part-time homeowners
- **Economic impact**: Fewer residents means fewer customers for local businesses year-round

## Relationship to STRs

Some dark homes are also used as [[short-term-rentals]], generating income for owners and [[bed-tax]] revenue but not providing stable housing. Others sit truly vacant. Both categories reduce the pool of housing available to the workforce.

## Policy responses

Possible policy tools include [[deed-restrictions]] on new development, STR regulations, and occupancy requirements. These are debated at the [[girdwood-board-of-supervisors]] and [[anchorage-assembly]] levels.
