## Overview

Deed restrictions are legal covenants attached to property deeds that limit how a property may be used. In [[girdwood]], deed restrictions are primarily discussed as a tool for ensuring that new housing developments serve the workforce and long-term residents rather than being converted to [[short-term-rentals]] or second homes.

## Purpose in Girdwood

Given Girdwood's housing challenges — [[dark-homes]], STR conversions, and a tight [[rental-market]] — deed restrictions are seen as one of the most effective tools for creating and preserving workforce housing. Common provisions include:

- **Primary residency requirements**: Owners must occupy the property as their primary residence
- **Rental restrictions**: Limits on short-term rental use
- **Resale price caps**: Restrictions on resale prices to maintain affordability over time
- **Income qualifications**: Requirements that buyers meet income thresholds

## Application

Deed restrictions are most commonly applied to housing built on [[heritage-land-bank]] land, where the [[municipality-of-anchorage]] can attach conditions as part of the land disposal process. The Holtan Hills development proposal includes deed-restricted workforce housing.

## Debate

Supporters argue deed restrictions are essential for preserving housing for the people who live and work in Girdwood. Critics raise concerns about limiting property rights, enforcement challenges, and long-term effects on property values.
