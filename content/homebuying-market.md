## Overview

The homebuying market in [[girdwood]] is characterized by high prices, limited inventory, and competition from second-home buyers and investors. Homeownership is out of reach for many people who work in the community.

## Prices

Home prices in Girdwood are significantly higher than the Anchorage average, reflecting the community's desirability as a resort and recreation destination. Median home prices have increased substantially over time, driven by demand from buyers outside the community.

## Demand drivers

Multiple demand sources compete for Girdwood's limited housing stock:

- **Second-home buyers**: Purchasers from Anchorage and beyond who want a vacation property near [[alyeska-resort]]
- **Investors**: Buyers who plan to operate [[short-term-rentals]]
- **Year-round residents**: Workers and families who want to live in Girdwood full-time

Because second-home buyers and investors often have greater purchasing power than local workers, they can outbid residents in a tight market.

## Affordability gap

The gap between local wages (see [[girdwood-economics-and-labor-market]]) and home prices means that many Girdwood workers cannot afford to buy in the community. This contributes to commuting patterns and limits community stability.

## Policy responses

- **[[Deed restrictions]]**: Requiring primary residency on new developments built on [[heritage-land-bank]] land
- **Workforce housing**: Proposed developments like Holtan Hills aim to create housing affordable to local workers
- **Down payment assistance**: Various programs may help bridge the affordability gap
