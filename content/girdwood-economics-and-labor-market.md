## Overview

[[Girdwood]]'s economy is dominated by tourism and recreation, centered on [[alyeska-resort]]. The community's labor market is characterized by seasonal employment patterns, a heavy reliance on service-sector jobs, and a persistent mismatch between where people work and where they can afford to live.

## Major employers

- **[[Alyeska Resort]]**: The largest employer, operating the ski area, Hotel Alyeska, and associated restaurants and facilities
- **[[Pomeroy]]**: Parent company of the resort
- **Local businesses**: Restaurants, retail shops, guiding and recreation companies, and property management firms
- **Girdwood Valley Service Area**: Public-sector employment through the [[girdwood-service-area]]

## Seasonal employment

Employment in Girdwood follows strong seasonal patterns. The ski season (November–April) and summer tourism season (June–September) are peak periods. The shoulder seasons see reduced employment and economic activity.

Seasonal workers face particular challenges finding housing, as landlords may prefer short-term vacation rentals over seasonal leases. See [[short-term-rentals]] and [[rental-market]].

## Housing-economy connection

The lack of affordable housing is widely cited as the most significant constraint on Girdwood's economy. Businesses report difficulty recruiting and retaining workers who cannot find places to live in the community. This has led to interest in [[deed-restrictions]] and other tools to create workforce housing.

## Bed tax revenue

Tourism generates [[bed-tax]] revenue that funds local services and projects. This creates a tension between encouraging tourism (which drives STR growth) and preserving housing for residents.
